## Key Theme:

1. **Scaling AI Content Is Risky**
AI-generated content can cause short-term traffic gains followed by severe and long-lasting visibility declines.

2. **Search Engines Detect Manipulation**
Google and Bing have sophisticated systems to identify AI abuse and artificial content refreshing.

3. **Sustainable SEO Matters**
Protecting long-term visibility is more important than chasing quick wins.

### Full Post:
This is something I’ve been paying close attention to over the last few weeks, as I’ve noticed multiple big brands seeing their organic traffic crash since mid-January.

I’ve also reviewed various new AI search case studies involving automating and scaling content creation and I’ve seen the same pattern Peec AI observed in their new article (linked below):

The sites may surge in traffic for a bit, then see substantial declines a few months later. These drops can be *really hard to recover from.* Recovery can also take a really long time - sometimes years.

Scaling AI content quickly is dangerous! It can also be dangerous to do things like artificially refreshing lots of content automatically for the sake of maintaining recent timestamps. 

Google and Bing have had systems in place for years to identify and demote sites abusing their content policies (which Bing just updated yesterday to include AI search manipulation, btw). Their systems are a lot more sophisticated than many people seem to realize…

If I sound like a broken record on these topics it’s because I work with the companies who learned the hard way that these tactics are unsustainable. It’s not pretty. The best way to drive longterm SEO (and AI search!) success is to not do things that risk destroying your visibility in the first place.

Check out the new research by Tomek Rudzki and Malte Landwehr from Peec AI - linked in the comments.

## Insights:

* Mass AI content scaling can backfire.
* Recovery from algorithmic drops is difficult.
* Artificial freshness tactics are risky.
* Long-term SEO requires quality and sustainability.

## Key Themes:

1. **Readers Dislike Obvious AI Content**
75% of respondents said they dislike content that is clearly AI-generated.
2. **Human Oversight Still Matters**
Low-quality AI content can hurt both SEO performance and audience trust.
3. **Content Quality Affects Brand Perception**
Success isn't just about rankings—reader experience matters too.

### Full Post:

How do readers feel about reading AI content?

Last week I ran a poll on LinkedIn & X to see how respondents felt about reading content that they could *just tell* was written with AI.

Out of 1,572 total responses, 75% claimed that they don't like reading AI content. Only 12% don't mind it.

It's important to note that the responses might be slightly skewed, as most of my followers/readers are SEOs, digital marketers, publishers and/or site owners who might dislike AI content, compared to the average reader. 

But... I still think it's worth keeping this in mind if your site is considering producing AI content with little human oversight. Not only is it risky from an SEO perspective but... your readers simply might not like it.

![Poll Result](/research/photos/lily-ray-2.jpeg)

## Insights:

* Users value authenticity.
* Human editing remains important.
* AI content can create trust issues.
* AI should support, not replace, human expertise.
